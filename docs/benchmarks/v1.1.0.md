# GridGuide v1.1.0 — final evidence report

Release-candidate report. Four kinds of evidence below answer different questions;
none establishes real-world restoration accuracy, deployment safety or utility impact.
The numerical studies remain frozen. Phase 9 refreshes regression/package verification
and reproduces G2; it does not retune or rerun G3/G5 to select better outcomes.

## A. Regression correctness

Four original source SHA256 pins, golden person-hour arithmetic, source identity/circuit
validation, complete reverse membership, missing evidence, API contracts and original
defaults remain protected. Synthetic scheduling uses an independent feasibility/metric
validator and a bounded exhaustive small-instance oracle. Tests reject precedence/crew
conflicts, duplicated customers, early release and falsely finite outcomes. Passing these
checks means the declared model is implemented consistently, not that assumed durations
or topology are empirically representative.

[Current verification](../planning/V1_1_0_PHASE9.md) distinguishes freshly executed checks
from [historical v1.0.0 evidence](../releases/VERIFICATION.md). Original labor is AEP
direct labor × 4.5 **once**, yielding person-hours, not elapsed time. Actual productivity
remains unknown. Independently authored fictional effort is not remultiplied by 4.5.

## B. Synthetic optimization performance

**Keep the strongest fixed release/person-hour baseline as the recommended default.**
The preregistered **5% median CP-SAT promotion target failed**: the observed median was
0%, with a limited cluster-bootstrap median interval [0%, 0%].

| Best-of-four incumbent plus CP-SAT versus fixed baseline | **0.351%** | 0% | 30 / 195 / 0 |
| Incremental gain beyond per-instance best-of-four | **0.174%** | 0% | 27 / 198 / 0 |

These use different denominators and **are not additive**. The first number cannot be
attributed entirely to CP-SAT. Optional experimental comparison is justified by validated
feasibility, not broad superiority. The public product uses only the pinned G3 schedules;
it does not solve new CP-SAT requests.

The unchanged G2 suite comprises six structural families × 10/25/100 tasks × 1/2/4 crews
× seeds **101, 211, 307, 401, 503** = **270 evaluation cases**, plus **eight microcases**.
Four baselines produce **1,112 policy runs**: FIFO, customer-count, release/person-hour
and critical-proxy-first adapters. The last two are explicitly normalized benchmark
adaptations; exact original four-candidate behavior has its own 24-case regression lane.
Development used seeds 17/29 and correctness cases. The published suite was already
inspected: **no blind final holdout exists**.

For disjoint customer groups, completed customer-hours are Σ n_g T_g, where T_g is the
last required repair's completion after the common modeled response origin. This is not
the entire observed outage duration. The 45 blocked-root evaluation cases have null full
completion; they are excluded only from the *finite* analysis. Restricted customer-hours
include **all 270 cases**, integrate waiting through 168 hours, and retain unresolved
mass without assigning it a finite ETR. Known completion beyond 168 hours is separately
censored, not unresolved. Restricted mean paired gains are 0.292530% versus fixed and
0.144697% beyond best-of-four. Every case, tie, failure disposition and negative secondary
effect remains in the [case-level results](phase3/published/results.jsonl.gz).

The fixed comparator was selected by lowest mean completed cost on common finite G2
cases, with policy-ID tie-breaking. Paired uncertainty resamples structural-family and
seed labels, retaining correlated scale/crew variants together: 5,000 crossed-cluster
resamples, seed 20261103. The fixed-comparator mean interval is
[0.0066916%, 1.1119194%]. Five finite families and five seeds support limited design
sensitivity interpretation, not calibrated population inference or independent-variant
sampling. See the [preregistered protocol](../research/phase3/EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md).

## C. Synthetic simulation sensitivity

G5 is **fixed-timetable delay propagation**, not adaptive dispatch: assignments and crew
order remain fixed, and sampled repairs cannot start before their original planned
start (plus an explicitly sampled hold). Policies receive common random draws and never
inspect future sampled durations. No empirical restoration outcomes fitted these rules.

Seven profiles are preserved: deterministic; independent lognormal σ=.25/.5; shared-event
lognormal σ=.25/.5 (half log variance shared); shared-.5 plus Uniform[0,4h) storm hold;
and independent Uniform[.5,1.5) multipliers as a misspecification sensitivity. Lognormal
median is one, but mean exceeds one. A storm hold supplies no unknown clearance and adds
no duplicate travel/setup. Distribution/correlation choices affect tails and paired
tradeoffs; they are assumptions, not measured utility productivity.

The standard study uses 278 cases × seven profiles × five fixed policies/references ×
2,000 trials. Convergence compares nested 2,000/10,000-trial prefixes on six finite
microcases × seven profiles × five policies = **210 combinations**. Of these, **195
were stable and 15 unstable at the cap**; all 15 diagnostics remain committed. Each
p10/p50/p90 must change by no more than max(0.25 h, 2% of the 10,000-trial quantile).
Microcase success does not establish convergence of the larger study. Numerical batch
errors and synthetic generating-process checks are not empirical interval coverage.

Python 3.12.14 / NumPy 2.5.4, PCG64, keyed streams and recorded seeds bind reproducibility.
Same-environment numerical replay was verified in G5. Cross-platform bitwise equivalence
and empirical calibration remain unverified. Complete regenerable trial caches live
outside Git; committed reports, manifests, numerical hashes and 56 fictional presets
remain available. Unresolved completion mass never becomes finite; unstable quantiles
must not be presented as converged. G5 visualization is **not in the final product**.

## D. Real-data predictive findings

G4 concluded an **evidence-bounded No-Go**. No validated household outage-probability
model was produced and no predictive performance is claimed. The challenge history
lacks usable outage identifiers, controls and customer restoration outcomes. EAGLE-I
outage observations were not acquired into the reviewed evidence; coverage and data-use
permissions therefore could not pass the gate. Acquired NOAA storm context is not an
outage label and retrospective severity cannot silently become a forecast feature.

Missing observations are not verified zeros. Real-data event split/leakage validation
was **not executed**; artificial protocol tests and ledger replay cannot substitute for
it. No model was fitted and no calibration earned. This does not prove that public-data
prediction is universally infeasible. Reopening requires a new versioned evidence review
and data-quality gate, never manual relabeling of the existing No-Go ledger.
